This embedded control software, developed by Daniel and Andrew for Team 09 in Lab Section 005, operates an automated multi-axis ping-pong ball launcher and targeting robot. Designed to run on an Arduino microcontroller, the sketch integrates precise mechanical actuation, optical position sensing, and real-time serial communication with MATLAB to execute complex projectile tasks. It functions as both a comprehensive hardware testing suite and a fully realized autonomous competition control program.

The system's hardware is meticulously mapped across digital and analog pins to manage distinct electromechanical functions. Linear stage motion is powered via PWM and direction pins (Digital 4 and 5), while high-speed ball discharge is triggered by an electromagnetic solenoid connected to Digital Pin 6. Absolute physical boundaries are monitored using left and right limit switches on Digital Pins 11 and 12, whereas an infrared sensor on Analog Pin A5 tracks optical stripe transitions to maintain precise position tracking, and an analog button array on Pin A7 captures user inputs.

To achieve smooth, responsive operation without the performance bottlenecks of traditional blocking delays, the software relies on a non-blocking state machine architecture. Functions such as AtHome, AtTarget, AtReloader, Moving, and PreparingToMove operate alongside debounced button and switch handlers to manage asynchronous events. This structure allows the microcontroller to simultaneously track elapsed time intervals, evaluate sensor inputs, and adjust servo angles for barrel elevation and ball reloading without stalling execution.

A standout feature of the codebase is its robust kinematic integration with MATLAB, enabling sophisticated downrange targeting calculations. Through specific serial commands, the Arduino requests computed trajectory parameters, processing mathematical functions like LandingDistance, LaunchAngle, and ThetaServo to determine optimal launch angles and servo positions. This computational pipeline accounts for initial velocities and geometrical constraints, allowing the robot to accurately aim and fire projectiles across multiple target distances.

Operational control and debugging are streamlined through an interactive serial menu operating at 9600 baud. By entering character keys ranging from 'a' to 'v', the operators can independently test individual hardware subsystems—such as checking IR LEDs, switch states, motor encoder counts, and reloader mechanics—or initiate the full autonomous competition routine. This structured interface provides complete transparency and diagnostic control over the entire mechatronic assembly during testing and deployment.
